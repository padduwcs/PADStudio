# Result review

## Use when

A result or artifact must be checked before continuation, delivery, or approval.

## Method

1. Read the target, its purpose, relevant brief/direction, and the producing run.
2. Evaluate technical integrity and creative effectiveness separately.
3. When a result assembles or transforms other results (concatenation, reformatting, a still pulled from a clip), check continuity and consistency at each seam — not only the final output in isolation.
4. For code animation, inspect every logical beat and transition. Use cumulative black/freeze evidence
   and adaptive timeline samples to locate suspicious ranges, then judge those ranges against narration
   and intent instead of failing them mechanically. Compare expected speech and important terms with ASR
   when available, and never describe sampled-frame inspection as a complete watch.
5. For an exact video Result, record `inspection` with the actual visual and audio methods used, concrete evidence, and any limits. Frame samples establish states; motion samples establish trajectories; audio analysis or ASR does not establish what a listener hears. A positive creative review needs motion evidence and actual listening when the render has audio. Use `passed_with_notes` when coverage remains sampled, and name its limits. Do not claim continuous playback or listening unless it actually occurred.
6. For a video or a narration, review it as the viewer the brief names, seeing it for the first time and not knowing the
   answer. Go through it in order and write down what that person hears, sees and wonders at the moments that matter:
   the start, each change of idea, the end. Note where they could get lost: a term used before it was explained, two
   things asking for attention at once, a picture that appears before the words that explain it, a step whose reason
   was never given. Write the criteria in those terms ("by this point the viewer knows why only one half is computed")
   and not only as a restatement of the specification ("thirteen cells are shown"). Meeting the brief is necessary;
   it is not the same as the viewer understanding.
7. For each criterion, record evidence and a status.
8. Every failure must include a timestamp/frame and a concrete proposed action; scan for repetitions
   of the same defect before asking for a revision.
9. Record a verdict; do not silently repair or approve on the user's behalf.

## Standard

Passing technical checks does not prove creative success. A result that satisfies every requirement but leaves the viewer unable to say what it taught has not succeeded. Subjective feedback must still be tied to stated intent and observable evidence.
